# Autonomous-Wall-Climbing-Robot
ESP32-based autonomous wall-climbing robot using BLDC aerodynamic adhesion.

An ESP32-based autonomous wall-climbing robot developed as part of the EEE 2124: Electronics course at United International University (UIU). The project investigates aerodynamic adhesion, where a high-speed BLDC motor generates downward thrust to create sufficient normal force, allowing the robot to adhere to and climb vertical surfaces while maintaining traction through its four-wheel differential drive system.
Inspired by the research paper EJBot-II: An Optimized Skid-Steering Propeller-Type Climbing Robot with Transition Mechanism, this project integrates embedded systems, robotics, motor control, sensor fusion, and mechanical design to demonstrate an alternative approach to wall climbing without relying on magnets or suction cups.
Throughout development, the system evolved from an initial dual-BLDC concept into a lightweight single-BLDC prototype through iterative design, testing, and optimization. The final prototype successfully validated the feasibility of aerodynamic wall adhesion through real-world testing and demonstrated controlled climbing on non-magnetic vertical surfaces.
